How the model is wired

Two groups of neurons, RP1 and CB, take turns. RP1 flashes in a steady rhythm and slowly stretches the body; CB fires in short bursts that make the body contract quickly, and while it fires RP1 goes quiet. Light speeds up or slows down RP1 depending on how used to it the animal is, and electrical pulses trigger CB. Repeated stimuli change the animal's state (habituation), which is what the readouts and graphs track.

Reading the readouts under the animal

  • Recruited: the share of each ensemble's cells that are allowed to fire. Every cell has a fixed threshold between 0 and 1 and is recruited when the ensemble's recruitment gain (gR for RP1, gC for CB) is above it. It is not the share firing at that instant: RP1 cells fire only in pulses while CB is silent, and CB cells only during a burst.
  • Body length: from 10% (the deepest possible contraction) to 100% (fully elongated, which is rest length plus the measured 41% elongation). Rest is about 64%.
  • Habituation, LS: hLS, from 0 (naive) to 1. It rises by 26% of the remaining gap for each light trial in which RP1 takes over, and relaxes back with the RP1 recovery time.
  • Habituation, ES: 1 minus the CB recruitment target (Ncb), so it starts at 0 and rises like LS, up to 0.6. It rises each time an electrical pulse drives a CB burst (the target falls by a factor of 0.909), but not in paired trials, where RP1 takes over and CB stays silent. It relaxes back with the CB recovery time. Actual CB recruitment follows the target with a lag of about 96 s.

The equations

Time inside the model runs on two clocks: the slow RP1 and CB equations advance by one model unit for every 3.212 s, while the RP1 pacemaker and everything in the animation run in real seconds. Variables are explained in the table below.

RP1 and CB competing (slow dynamics)
yR = max(0, xR),   yC = max(0, xC)
τrR dxR/dt = −xR − aCRyC − bRvR + sR + LS·gdrive(1 − 0.5h)
τaR dvR/dt = −vR + yR
τrC dxC/dt = −xC − aRCyR − ρ·tick − bCvC + sC + ES·gdrive
τaC dvC/dt = −vC + yC
aRC = abase + aLS·LS·(1 − h),   tick = sin6(φ) while RP1 is on,   dφ/dt = 2π·f0(1 + gLS·LS·(1 − h))
How many cells are allowed to fire (recruitment)
τg dgR/dt = −gR + (1 − κgC)
τg dgC/dt = −gC + Ncb
a cell is recruited when its threshold θi (random, 0 to 1) is below g
RP1 rhythm and the light effect
fRP1 = (0.109 + 0.027h) + L·(0.015 − 0.051h)   Hz
dL/dt = (LS − L)/τL,   τL = 2 s while rising, 80 s while falling
Habituation and recovery
LS:   h → 1 − (1 − h)·e−0.30 after each light trial where RP1 takes over;   dh/dt = −h/τH
ES:   Ncb → max(0.40, 0.909·Ncb) for each electrical pulse that drives a CB burst and is not overridden by RP1 (judged over the first 6 s);   dNcb/dt = (1 − Ncb)/τCB;   ES habituation = 1 − Ncb
CB burst rate: τaR is multiplied by a factor that falls from 1.0 (h = 0) to 0.36 (h = 1), raising the burst rate about 47%
Neurons and fluorescence
RP1 spike rate = 20·ki·sin6(φ + δi)   Hz   (recruited cells, while CB is quiet)
CB spike rate = min(30, 14·ki·xC1.5)   Hz   (recruited cells, during a burst)
da/dt = −a/τdecay + spikes,   db/dt = −b/τrise + spikes,   c = NK(a − b)
ΔF/F = G·cn / (cn + Kn)
Body length
contraction depth D = min(60, 34.1·xCgC / 0.6422) %, reached within about 0.6 s while CB is active
each RP1 pulse:   T → T + 0.22(E − T),   T ≤ 41;   body length follows T through two 1.7 s lags
target E = 36 + L·(ELS − 36),   ELS = 60 − 38h   (spontaneous oscillation off: E = L·(80 − 36h))
body-length graph = 50 + 50·change/41 % for elongation, 50 + 50·change/60 % for contraction (50% is rest)
body-length readout = 10 + 90·(change + 60)/101 %,   where change is the percent change from rest
