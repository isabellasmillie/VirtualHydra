Hydra neuroanatomy

The shape of the animal and where its neurons sit are approximated from measurements of real Hydra vulgaris. The two cell groups in this model, RP1 and CB, are two of the three body-spanning networks described by Dupre & Yuste; their counts, density along the body, soma size and processes follow that paper, and the body and tentacle shapes follow photographs and published tentacle anatomy. Everything here is a visual approximation, not a measured reconstruction.

How the model is wired

Two groups of neurons, RP1 and CB, take turns. RP1 flashes in a steady rhythm and slowly stretches the body; CB fires in short bursts that make the body contract quickly, and while it fires RP1 goes quiet. Light speeds up or slows down RP1 depending on how used to it the animal is, and electrical pulses trigger CB. Repeated stimuli change the animal's state (habituation), which is what the readouts and graphs track.

Reading the readouts under the animal

  • RP1 active / CB active (top left): "CB active" lights while CB is bursting. "RP1 active" lights whenever RP1 is allowed to flash, which is whenever CB is not bursting (with spontaneous oscillation off, only after light or while the body is returning to its middle length). "Co-active" lights when RP1 and CB are both tonically on at once for more than 12 s, which happens when RP1's inhibition of CB is weak (see Circuit asymmetry). It never lights at the default setting.
  • RP1 rhythm: the pacemaker frequency of the RP1 flashing, 0.109 Hz at rest when naive. After light it rises in a naive animal and falls in a habituated one.
  • Recruited: the share of each ensemble's cells that are allowed to fire. Every cell has a fixed threshold between 0 and 1 and is recruited when the ensemble's recruitment gain (gR for RP1, gC for CB) is above it. It is not the share firing at that instant: RP1 cells fire only in pulses while CB is silent, and CB cells only during a burst.
  • Body length: from 10% (the deepest possible contraction) to 100% (fully elongated, which is rest length plus the measured 41% elongation). Rest is about 64%.
  • Habituation, LS: hLS, from 0 (naive) to 1. It rises by 11% of the remaining gap for each light trial in which RP1 takes over, and relaxes back with the RP1 recovery time. In natural mode, a light that arrives while CB has the floor does not count; with spontaneous oscillation off, every light counts.
  • Habituation, ES: 1 minus the CB recruitment target (Ncb), so it starts at 0 and rises like LS, up to 0.6. It rises each time an electrical pulse drives a CB burst (the target falls by a factor of 0.909), but not in paired trials, where RP1 takes over and CB stays silent. It relaxes back with the CB recovery time. Actual CB recruitment follows the target with a lag of about 96 s.

Reading the graphs

  • CB ΔF/F and RP1 ΔF/F: the average simulated fluorescence of each ensemble (GCaMP6s or 6f), scaled so naive peaks match the measured 4.99 (CB) and 0.325 (RP1).
  • CB Hz: how often CB bursts, drawn as 1 divided by the time between the last two bursts and held until the next one. The axis rescales to fit (0 to 0.008 Hz up to 0 to 0.1 Hz), and faint lines mark 0.00364 and 0.00536 Hz.
  • RP1 Hz: the pacemaker frequency of the RP1 flashing, with a gap while CB is bursting. Faint lines mark 0.109 and 0.124 Hz.
  • Body length (%): 0 to 100%, with the gray line at 50% for rest. Olive above the line is elongation, and blue below it is contraction.
  • Habituation: LS (amber) and ES (blue) habituation. Each steps up with trials and drifts back down with recovery. ES can only reach 0.6.
  • Shaded bands: amber when light is on, blue at each electrical pulse.

The circuit view

  • The Circuit button swaps the animal for your circuit diagram. The readouts and graphs keep running.
  • The RP1 and CB bubbles flash with their own spikes, and the thick amber RP1-to-CB inhibition branch flashes while light and electrical stimuli overlap (paired). That branch also gets thicker or thinner with the Circuit asymmetry slider.
  • The light and electrical input arrows glow while that stimulus is delivered.
  • "Repeated LS" and the Timing Axis glow more as LS habituation grows. "Repeated ES" and the Recruitment Axis do the same for ES habituation. Both fade as habituation recovers.

Using the controls

  • Stimulate: light lasts 10 s and the electrical pulse 300 ms (both adjustable under "Stimulus lengths"). Paired starts the electrical pulse halfway through the light, 5 s in.
  • Training Paradigms: LS Habituation (15 LS), ES Habituation (5 ES), Classical Conditioning (15 paired), LS/ES Alternating (10 rounds), all 120 s apart, or Custom. The bubbles fill in as each stimulus is delivered.
  • Playback: 1×, 5×, 20× (the default) or 60× real time.
  • Habituation State: the sliders set LS and ES habituation directly. "Natural spontaneous oscillation" switches the animal's own rhythm on or off: when off, the animal hovers at its middle length, with no spontaneous CB bursts, until a stimulus arrives. "Reset to naive" clears both habituation values.
  • Recovery settings: CB recovery (15 min) and RP1 recovery (8 hours) set how quickly ES and LS habituation fade. "Live recovery" lets stimuli change the habituation values as they run; unchecked, the values stay where you put them.
  • Circuit asymmetry: scales how strongly RP1 inhibits CB (×1.00 is the data-based setting). Weaker inhibition makes CB bursts more frequent; stronger makes them rarer. The text under the slider says whether the circuit is still alternating, and the ratio shows RP1→CB strength relative to CB→RP1. When inhibition is weak enough that RP1 and CB are on together, the "Co-active" light comes on and extra CB cells in the foot twinkle alongside the flashing RP1 cells. Changing it does not alter the paired rule (RP1 always wins) or the habituation rules.
  • Display: GCaMP6s or 6f sets the indicator speed. Fluorescence shows the smoothed signal and Spikes shows each spike. Green or By ensemble sets cell color. These affect the animal view only.

The equations

Time inside the model runs on two clocks: the slow RP1 and CB equations advance by one model unit for every 3.212 s, while the RP1 pacemaker and everything in the animation run in real seconds. Variables are explained in the table below.
